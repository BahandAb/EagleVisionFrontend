Our Story

Built in a Classroom.
For Every Classroom.

EagleVision started with a simple observation: only one person can look through a microscope at a time. This is the story of how that changed.

Patent Pending

What EagleVision Is

EagleVision is a live microscopy streaming platform. A single microscope streams to every device in the room at once, so students can see the slide in real time, annotate together, and use AI-assisted analysis. No hardware upgrades needed.

Microscopes are one-to-one by design. One instructor, one field of view, thirty students waiting for a turn. EagleVision makes it a shared experience instead of a sequential one.

How It Started

EagleVision started in Dr. Harsh's AP Biology class at Walnut Hills High School. Preparing microscope samples for 30 students while managing a full schedule is genuinely hard, and when a lab fell apart one day, she had to make do with projected images that students in the back could barely see. Bahand had already been tinkering with a Raspberry Pi camera to reduce his own eye strain from long hours at the microscope. Dr. Harsh had gifted him a 1000x unit, and watching that lab struggle made the connection click. The problem he had been quietly solving for himself was a classroom-wide problem. He brought the idea to Mr. Kerkhoff, who encouraged him to go ahead and see where it went.

The first version ran on a Raspberry Pi, streaming a live feed to a local network. From there it evolved into a phone app and web platform, accessible without any dedicated hardware. When the team piloted EagleVision at Madeira Elementary, Mrs. Marketos noted that it required no prep from the teacher. She could think on her feet and adjust on the fly, which is how a live classroom actually works. Every student engaged with the same feed in their own way, including IEP students, 504s, ELLs, and gifted learners. The team brought that experience to the 2026 UC IT Expo, where they competed against college teams. Their judges, Abdou and William, understood the problem immediately. Abdou was paraplegic; William was from Ghana. Both had personal context for what it means when access to tools is the limiting factor. The team scored 104 rubric points. No other high school team broke 100. They won Best of the Best overall and first place in the high school division.

The provisional patent was filed on January 5, 2026 (application 64/028,055), covering the core system for distributed real-time microscopy broadcasting and client-side image processing. After the Expo, judge William pulled Mr. Kerkhoff aside and asked for his contact information. He said EagleVision was remarkable and wanted to connect the team with the Ghanaian government to explore bringing it into their schools. Around the same time, a local medical professional reached out to say the technology would be useful in clinical settings too. A fix for one difficult lab day had grown into something bigger.
